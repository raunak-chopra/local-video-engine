# Directed motion mechanics study

Purpose: demonstrate explicit scene edges and a reproducible review schedule.
Audience: maintainers of the personal video engine. No factual product claims,
recipe instructions or audience-performance claims appear in the copy.

Output: eight seconds, 30 fps, 1080-square, silent. Vertical and landscape
opening stills are layout spot checks, not full-canvas acceptance of the film.

Visual thesis: the picture demonstrates the words. The opening and final idea
are already present on their first frame. The middle statement moves into place;
the list appears complete rather than delaying its supporting items.

Look: dark blue/gray, warm off-white copy and amber emphasis; bundled Inter.
Large primary text, small numbered eyebrow, stable left alignment and generous
space. The existing engine backdrop is retained. This fixture demonstrates
controls and QA; it is not presented as a polished gallery-showreel competitor.

| Scene | Output range | Treatment and acceptance intention |
|---|---|---|
| opening | [0, 60) | Cut in/out; full headline and eyebrow on first/last frame. |
| progress | [60, 120) | Existing spring/fade entrance, hard exit; inspect near-settled copy at 100. The empty initial frame is deliberate for this mode. |
| proof | [120, 180) | Cut entrance, fade exit; every list item immediately visible. |
| payoff | [180, 240) | Cut in/out; title and CTA held through frame 239. |

Timing grid: synthetic 120 BPM, four beats/bar, offset 0. There is no supplied
song, beat detection, generated score or evidence of music synchronization.
The grid only demonstrates planning and sample generation. The film is not
intended to loop; its last frame deliberately differs from the opening.

Revision policy: keep the four scene ranges stable for mechanics comparisons.
If copy changes, inspect all affected holds and boundaries. For an artistic
showreel, create a new brief with a subject and original visual thesis instead
of adding gratuitous effects to this fixture.

Next editorial acceptance on an actual video: source-backed subject matter,
readable script/overlays, full-speed playback, sound if selected and phone review.
