# Reel playbook (cooking, faceless, music + text)

Status: DRAFT from public research on 2026-10-13. Instagram publishes no
official benchmarks. Most numbers below come from third-party blogs and are
rules of thumb, not facts. Treat every item as a hypothesis and A/B test it on
your own account. Your own Insights history is the only reliable benchmark.

## 1. What Instagram says it ranks on

Best-supported (Mosseri, January 2025, as quoted by secondary sources):
- Top three signals: **watch time, likes, sends**.
- Likes matter slightly more for people who already follow you; **sends
  (DM shares) matter slightly more for reaching new people**.
- Watch time counts both percentage watched and seconds watched, so longer
  videos are not penalised for length alone.
- Insights to track per reel: **average watch time, likes per reach, sends per
  reach**, plus **skip rate** (viewers who leave inside the first 3 seconds).

Not confirmed officially: saves as a top signal, exact weights ("sends are 3-5x
likes"), and any January 2026 "new hierarchy". Use as directional only.

## 2. Hook (first 2-3 seconds)

- Show the finished or most striking food frame immediately, with sizzle or a
  hit sound on frame 0. Do not open on a logo, title card or slow fade.
- Put a bold on-screen text hook in the first second, readable muted.
- Hook patterns (templates, not proven):
  - Number + time + proof: "3 cheezein. 15 minute. Ghar waale roz maangte hain."
  - Mistake: "Tum biryani galat bana rahe ho" / "Aap dal galat bana rahe ho"
  - Contrast: "Same masala, alag result"
  - Curiosity gap: "Ye ek cheez daalo, taste double"
  - Relatable meme setup for the 3-10 s format
- Judge a hook by **skip rate**, not likes.

## 3. Retention and story tension

- Structure: **hook -> process with tension -> payoff**. Keep the payoff
  (finished plated shot) hidden or teased until the end; open a loop early
  ("wait for the last step") and close it late.
- Tension tools: countdown or timer overlay, "step 3 changes everything",
  a quick preview flash of the result, a mistake-then-fix beat.
- Cut every 1-2 seconds; change something visual (angle, zoom, text) at least
  every 2-3 seconds. Sync cuts and text pops to the music beat.
- Read the retention graph: a steep early drop means a weak hook; a slow fade
  means weak pacing or a flat middle.
- A 30 s reel at 50% average retention (15 s watched) beats a 10 s reel at 80%
  (8 s watched) for watch time. Do not chase percentage alone.
- Length guidance (blogs, not official): 7-15 s for reach and new followers,
  15-60 s for recipes. Your usual 30-60 s is fine for recipes; the 3-10 s meme
  format is the reach and recall play.

## 4. Loops and rewatches

- Make the last frame visually match the first so the reel restarts smoothly
  (end on the same shot, sound or text as the opening).
- Repetitive cooking actions (stirring, chopping, pouring) loop naturally.
- Rewatches raise average watch time, so a short loopable cut can outperform
  a longer one.

## 5. Sound and ASMR

- Close-up cooking sounds (sizzle, chop, pour, crunch) layered under music.
- Music with a clear beat; text and cuts land on it.
- Always design for sound-off too: text must carry the meaning.
- Trending audio is a separate lever; pick it from Instagram's own audio tab at
  posting time. Licensed Envato music may not be eligible for that; the two
  strategies can run side by side.

## 6. On-screen text (Hindi / Hinglish / English)

- Large, high-contrast, inside the safe area (64 px minimum margin; keep clear
  of the bottom caption area and right-side buttons).
- Max about 7 words per line, 2 lines. One idea per screen.
- Match the script to the audience: Devanagari for emotional, traditional or
  heritage dishes; Hinglish for casual, trendy, meme formats. Test each.
- Ingredient lists and step numbers on screen make the reel saveable.

## 7. Sends and saves (the growth levers)

- Design for **sends**: relatable meme moments, "send this to someone who...",
  "tag your mom/roommate". A line like "Ye kisi ko bhejo jo roz Maggi banata
  hai" in the last second.
- Design for **saves**: full recipe on screen, ingredient card at the end.
- A clear, light call to action in the final 1-2 seconds, in the same
  language as the reel.

## 8. Originality: important for the meme plan

Instagram stopped recommending accounts that repost unoriginal content (rule
from 2024-2025, expanded to photos and carousels on 2026-04-30). Adding your
own commentary, analysis or original graphics can qualify; cropping, resizing,
watermarking or crediting does not.

Implication for meme templates: licensing a meme clip does not by itself make
the reel "original" in Instagram's eyes. Make each meme reel transformative:
your own footage, your own text and joke, your own edit and music, not a
re-upload of someone else's clip. Check Instagram's Account Status page for
your account's real standing.

## 9. Posting and testing routine

- Keep a log of every reel: hook text, length, music, skip rate, average
  watch time, sends per reach. Compare to your last 10 reels.
- Change one variable at a time (hook, length, music, text language).
- Post consistently; review Insights 24-48 hours after posting.
- Instagram trial reels (shown to non-followers first) are a safe place to
  test hooks; verify availability on your account.

## 10. What this means for the engine (build backlog)

| Feature | Why |
|---|---|
| Hook-first template: frame-0 food shot, SFX hit, text in first 20 frames | Skip rate |
| Beat-synced cuts and text pops | Retention |
| Countdown/timer overlay and "wait for it" teaser flash | Story tension |
| Loop-match ending (reuse first frame/sound at end) | Rewatches |
| Ingredient card and step badges scene | Saves |
| "Send this to..." end line component (Devanagari/Hinglish) | Sends |
| `meme` scene with setup, beat, punchline timing | 3-10 s recall format |
| Safe-area guard for bottom caption and right buttons | Text not covered by UI |
| Reel log file (`docs/reel-log.md` or JSON) | A/B testing |
