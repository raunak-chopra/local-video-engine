## A usable tagging convention

Keep the public `categories` field untouched. For an actively studied reference
or a project brief, record separate, evidence-linked facets in Markdown:

| Facet | Examples | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Source category | Original public category, or uncategorized | Catalog row/date |
| Creative mechanism | Recontextualization, transformation, contrast, reveal, continuity of attention, escalating reward | Explain how it works; distinguish prompt intent from observed film behavior |
| Story role | Hook, setup, progress, proof, payoff, invitation | Source scene/time or explicit authored plan |
| Visual treatment | Type hierarchy, material texture, negative space, palette roles, framing | Actual source image/time or labelled brief intention |
| Motion treatment | Hard editorial cut, persistent anchor, staged reveal, held image, morph | Source motion review; prompt-only intent is labelled as intent |
| Emotional intention | Warmth, tactile curiosity, humor, relief | Director's stated goal; viewer response remains unknown until observed |
| Engine support | Implemented, extension needed, source-dependent | Code path and version, not a reference's advertised stack |
| Review status | Indexed only, text reviewed, image reviewed, motion reviewed, listened, director accepted | Artifact/frame/time, reviewer/date and decision scope |

Example reference card: the [UI morph prompt](https://promptmotion.org/twoclipping-5cba86)
intends continuity of attention and state transformation. Its text is reviewed;
its full motion and sound were not reviewed in this audit. A cooking adaptation
could carry one ingredient through real transformations. Current clip trims and
focus support editorial continuity; continuous graphic morphing needs an
extension. Tagging it `morph` must not imply the engine has implemented morphing.

Example project card: `mechanism: transformation`, `feeling: tactile curiosity`,
`role: payoff`, `support: source-dependent`, `review: planned`. This is a proposed
direction until a designated lead chooses it. Do not label an output `premium`,
`viral`, `creative` or `approved` merely because its input had that tag.

This convention is an authoring/review discipline, not a new CLI search feature,
automatic classifier or expanded `project.json` schema. The unstudied catalog
rows remain unstudied; they have not received invented mechanism or quality tags.

## Creativity assessment

The current stock vocabulary is strong at presenting information and weak at
expressing bespoke visual relationships without code changes. Eight scene
names are not eight creative concepts. The same left alignment, radial wash,
scale relationships and reveal vocabulary recur. This is a useful base for
clear graphics but can flatten an incoming director's original idea.

Assess an execution by whether the intended relationship is visible: a
transformation should transform; a contrast should make the difference legible;
a choice should reveal alternatives and the consequence of choosing. Merely
writing those words on successive cards is insufficient when the director asked
for the relationship itself. This is a qualitative craft judgment, not a model
benchmark or engagement prediction.

Taste belongs to the designated creative lead. Ask for the intended degree of
restraint, playfulness, material detail, contrast, density and cultural tone when
these are genuinely open. Present concrete executions and tradeoffs; do not
turn a fashion such as gradients, grain, giant type or constant movement into
a universal definition of quality. Review craft failures against the chosen
direction, and keep preference disagreements separate from clipping, wrong
numbers and unreadable exposure.

The new [option menu](CREATIVE-OPTIONS.md) exposes editorial routes, every current
graphic scene, canvas/type/color controls, footage, timing, sound and extension
paths. Present the relevant options to any designated lead, state their costs,
then follow its choice. Do not take the lead back because the preferred concept
requires more work than a default scene.
