# Hooks, story tension, retention and reel growth

Research date: 13 October 2026. Applies to this engine's faceless cooking reels, with original footage, music and Hindi/Hinglish/English text. Companion to `REELS-RESEARCH-2026-10-13.md` and operational `REEL-PLAYBOOK.md`.

## What the evidence supports

There is no verified equation that guarantees virality. Use a creative hypothesis: **a relevant promise earns attention; visible progress earns continued viewing; a satisfying result earns trust; practical or social value earns sharing.** This is our editorial synthesis, not Instagram's ranking formula.

## Hook: give the viewer a reason to care

A strong opening combines a relevant visual, a specific promise or question, and evidence that the video can deliver it. Choose from the footage; do not force a text hook over an unrelated opening shot.

| Hook family | Example draft | Footage / source requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory result | “Is crunch ka secret?” / “इतना कुरकुरा कैसे?” | A clear crunch or texture reveal; the body must explain the relevant technique |
| Useful correction | “Crispy chahiye? Yeh step mat skip karo.” | A supplied, accurate technique and a visible demonstration |
| Constraint | “Bache hue chawal ka naya dinner.” | Actual leftover-rice recipe; do not invent price, ingredients or preparation time |
| Contrast | “Same ingredients. Dono ka texture alag kyun?” | Genuine comparison footage with comparable conditions |
| Relatable situation | “Jab fridge mein bas kal ke chawal bache hon…” | Own footage that resolves the situation, with an optional reaction accent |
| Process mystery | “Is batter se aisa texture kaise?” | Visible starting state and a clear end result |

Draft three meaningfully different openings before rendering variants. Pair each text line with a source range and name the exact promised payoff. Reject an opening if it invents results, exaggerates a recipe claim, or creates a question the footage cannot answer.

Opening target: make the subject and reason to watch apparent in the first moments. Exact text appearance, shot length and sound are creative choices. A visual-only opening is valid when it communicates enough. A mandatory loud sound hit, logo ban or fixed frame deadline would be too rigid.

## Tension: an unresolved question with visible progress

For cooking, tension can be simple: will the texture work, how does the dish transform, which step creates the result, or can these leftovers become something appealing? Stakes must be truthful and proportionate.

Use **setup → obstacle or contrast → turning point → payoff** when the material supports it. Each beat should advance the answer. Examples:

- Texture: show the desired crust, establish preparation, show the decisive technique, demonstrate the final texture.
- Mistake/fix: show a real failure, identify the supplied cause, demonstrate the correction, show the result. Do not manufacture a failure from unrelated footage.
- Transformation: establish an ordinary starting state, show important changes, hold the final reveal long enough to appreciate it.
- Relatable mini-story: establish the meal situation, show the solution taking shape, end with the finished dish and the joke resolved.

Showing the dish early does not eliminate tension: the open question can be **how to achieve it**. An end reveal also works when the evolving action is compelling. Avoid repeated “wait till the end” text, fake timers, or withholding every useful detail. Deliver small answers during the middle, then the main answer.

## Retention: cut by meaning, not by stopwatch

For every shot, identify its job: establish, demonstrate, reveal, compare, explain or reward. Remove shots that repeat the same information without adding sensory pleasure or necessary clarity.

- Enter near the meaningful action; leave after the viewer understands it. Keep enough handles for a readable pour, flip or reveal.
- Compress repetitive stirring/chopping while preserving the recipe sequence. Hold key techniques and the final sensory moment longer.
- Use a new angle, close-up, sound change, comparison or text emphasis when it advances the story. Visual changes every two seconds are not a verified requirement.
- Let cuts land near useful music beats when possible. Do not chop an action or an instruction merely to hit a beat.
- Use real cooking sounds selectively; briefly reduce music for a crunch or sizzle. Avoid placing a whoosh on every cut.
- Show one readable idea at a time. Preview on a phone with and without sound. Text exposure should match its reading burden, including Devanagari shaping and line breaks.
- Make a loop only if the ending is complete and the return feels natural. Do not truncate the answer to force a replay or make text unreadably fast.
- Reaction inserts are optional emotional beats. They should sharpen the joke or reveal; remove them if they interrupt understanding. Existing 0.5–2 second / 1–3 insert preferences are house heuristics, not platform rules.

## Payoff, sends, saves and follows

Give the viewer the promised result before any request. A recipe should be usable: supplied quantities and important steps can go in readable overlays or the post caption. Avoid cramming a full recipe into a flashing end card.

Design a reason to share: a recognizable household situation, a practical solution for a specific friend, an unusual but truthful result, or an enjoyable transformation. A “send this” line is optional; it cannot substitute for that reason.

Choose at most one primary action for a reel: save a useful recipe, share a relatable moment, or follow a clear series. A series should make the next value specific, such as another leftover-rice idea. Do not fabricate a forthcoming episode.

These are editorial recommendations. Saves may express utility, shares may express social value, and follows may express recurring interest; none of those interpretations establishes a fixed algorithm weight.

## Distribution techniques worth testing

1. Use a specific topic and audience need. Opening text, cover and caption should describe the actual dish/technique. Relevant searchable wording is a publishing hypothesis, not a guaranteed ranking boost.
2. Choose audio for the mood and edit; examine current trends only at posting time. Trending music does not guarantee reach. Keep a clean export so an in-platform soundtrack can be selected separately if appropriate.
3. Use a clear cover for profile browsing. The opening frame still needs to work in autoplay. Do not transfer YouTube thumbnail statistics into Instagram advice.
4. Test useful recurring formats and genuine collaborations with relevant audiences. Review result quality and audience fit instead of chasing an arbitrary daily posting quota.
5. Use the account's current Best Practices and Account Status screens for personalized guidance and recommendation eligibility. This research did not inspect the owner's account.
6. Compare publishing times using the owner's audience/activity data and several comparable posts. There is no verified universal “best time” here.
7. Treat hashtags, captions, Stories sharing and comment responses as supporting tactics. No verified magic count, guaranteed initial-engagement window, or boost from engagement pods was found in the reviewed primary sources.

Paid boosting is a separate distribution experiment. Paid impressions cannot be used as evidence that an organic hook or retention strategy improved. No paid promotion or publishing is authorized by this research request.

## Read retention as a diagnostic, not a verdict

| Observation | Candidate explanation | Next test |
|---|---|---|
| Early abandonment | Unclear visual/promise, audience mismatch, slow setup or unreadable text | Change opening shot or promise; review who received the reel |
| Drop at a specific cut | Confusing continuity, repetitive action, obstructing meme/text, audio discontinuity | Inspect that timestamp and change that beat |
| Gradual decline | Flat progression, excessive length, ordinary audience attrition | Remove redundant middle material and strengthen progress |
| Rewatch spike | Enjoyable moment, loop, confusing explanation or dense text | Watch the actual segment before calling it success |
| Good watching, weak sharing | Useful/pleasant but little social relevance, or wrong audience | Test a clearer recipient/situation rather than adding a generic CTA |
| Good reach, weak follows | One-off interest or unclear repeat value | Test a truthful series proposition and profile coherence |

Track the platform's exact definition and denominator. Report average watch seconds and length separately; average watch time divided by length is a descriptive ratio, not a completion rate. Replays can make it exceed 100%. A 30-second reel watched for 15 seconds does not automatically outrank a 10-second reel watched for 8 seconds.

Keep Instagram and YouTube metrics separate: [YouTube's Shorts documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en) distinguishes starts/replays from engaged views; its [performance documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) describes average duration among viewers who stayed to watch. Do not compare rates with incompatible denominators.

## Operational contract for the assistant

Before editing, read `REEL-PLAYBOOK.md` and use the following brief. Keep it with the reel project; these fields are a proposed authoring convention, not new engine schema support.

```text
Audience / situation:
Viewer value: sensory | useful | relatable | transformation
Source-backed recipe notes:
Opening candidates: [visual source range + text + promised payoff] x 3
Selected question or promise:
Story beats: [source range + role + why this comes next]
Turning point / partial answers:
Payoff source range:
Language / font / reading needs:
Music / natural-sound moments:
Optional meme insert: [reason; remove if distracting]
Primary viewer action, if any:
One variable to test:
Uncertainties / missing shots:
```

Before handoff, review the actual reel for promise/payoff alignment, action comprehension, truthful sequence, phone readability, sensory pacing and sound balance. Mark creative readiness with reasons; do not generate a “viral score.” Keep automated checks for missing media, trim bounds, font loading and frame math separate from editorial review.

For learning, log comparable reels at consistent observation ages, for example 24 hours, 72 hours and seven days. Record platform, format, duration, hook, paid/organic status, reach, metric definitions, watch time, retention if available, shares/sends as exposed, saves, follows and revision notes. Record absent metrics as unknown, not zero.

Change one major variable per comparison and repeat across more than one dish/topic. Use Trial Reels where available, but do not describe sequential trials as randomized A/B tests. Choose success metrics before posting; inspect sample size and audience mix. Save only user-approved preferences and observed results. Until those exist, improvements are creative hypotheses.
